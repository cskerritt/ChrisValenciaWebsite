## How far is Powerline Tattoo from Providence?

About ten minutes by car, and it's the same ten from most of the city. Route 10 leaves I-95 near the Cranston line and ends on Reservoir Avenue, which is Route 2; the shop is a short way down. No car? A rideshare from downtown is a short ride, and RIPTA's map is worth a look.

## Why leave Providence for a tattoo artist in Cranston?

Nobody leaves Providence for Cranston. They leave for a specific artist, and the city line happens to be in the way. Chris draws fun, surreal images with an animated, whimsical feel, works fully custom, and specializes in illustrative, neo-traditional and new-school. If that's the drawing you want, the ten minutes are the easy part.

Providence is a drawing town. Between RISD, the murals downtown and the flash on every second forearm on Thayer Street, people here know the difference between a template and a hand. Chris's portfolio reads like a sketchbook that got loose: a cardinal whose feathers dissolve into filigree, a plague-doctor raven, an orange with one wedge cut out and drawn so carefully you can count the seeds.

## What kind of tattoos does Chris do?

Custom illustrative, neo-traditional and new-school work, in color or black-and-grey, with a habit of combining things that shouldn't go together and making them look inevitable. Birds, animals, botanicals, pets, cartoon characters, objects with a story. He works all styles if you ask, but those three are where the portfolio is deepest.
